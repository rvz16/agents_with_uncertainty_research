\begin{frame}{Should a half-finished run be labelled by how it ended?}
\scriptsize
\begin{columns}[t]
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
To train a model that predicts whether an agent will succeed, we cut each
recorded attempt into prefixes and give every prefix the attempt's \emph{final}
outcome. That looks wrong: at step 10 the work may be going fine and still
collapse later, or be half-done and then thrown away.

\medskip
\textbf{So we bought the real answer.} A task verifier re-ran the repository
\emph{as it actually stood} at each step and returned a partial-credit score:
282 graded points over 76 coding episodes.

\medskip
\textbf{The two labels do disagree.} On \g{36\,\%} of shared points the graded
state differs from the final outcome --- 83 of them below it (progress not yet
made) and 14 above it: the agent had more tests passing mid-run than it
finished with.

\medskip
We then scored the whole 72-method uncertainty table twice --- identical
prefixes, folds and code, only the label's value changed.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
\begin{block}{The answer is no}
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
mean score & step label & final label \\
\midrule
all prefixes    & +0.14 & +0.12 \\
graded only     & +0.14 & \g{+0.22} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip
Swapping the label moves the average by \textbf{+0.017} one way and
\bad{$-$0.089} the other --- the sign depends on which way you cut the
prefixes. The ranking of the 72 methods barely moves ($\rho=0.76$--$0.82$) and
the same family stays on top.

\medskip
\textbf{Practical conclusion: per-step verifier labels are not worth
collecting.} One outcome per finished attempt is enough.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2
\begin{frame}{What we had to withdraw along the way}
\scriptsize
\begin{columns}[t]
\begin{column}{0.51\textwidth}
\textbf{1. The one positive number in it was an artefact.}

\smallskip
Our Bayesian teacher looked $+0.23$ better against the step label than against
the outcome --- which would have meant it measures progress, not forecasting.
On graded points only the gap is \g{+0.04}: the effect was \textbf{unequal
replication}, since carrying a label across unchanged steps duplicates some
episodes $5.9\times$. \bad{+0.23 is withdrawn.}

\medskip
\textbf{2. A leak underneath the whole table.}

\smallskip
The tool critics were computed over the \emph{whole episode} and stamped on
every step, so every critic-based method saw the future. On the 37 rows that
avoid them the signal averages about \textbf{zero}, best $+0.33$ against a
length baseline of $+0.24$. Every absolute number there is withdrawn until the
critics are recomputed per prefix. The A/B above survives --- both arms share
the construction.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textbf{3. The graded sample is not a random half.}

\smallskip
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & $n$ & mean score & median steps \\
\midrule
graded   & 79 & 0.414 & 64 \\
ungraded & 33 & \bad{0.722} & \bad{29} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip
The attempts we failed to grade are the short successful ones. Within-subset
comparison is unaffected, but no absolute number from this page can sit beside
the main table.

\medskip
\begin{block}{Three ways in, three negatives}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
as model \textbf{input} & $+0.73 \rightarrow -0.02$, retracted \\
as \textbf{augmentation} & $-0.01$ to $-0.26$ \\
as the \textbf{target} & $+0.017$ / $-0.089$ \\
\end{tabular}

\smallskip
Tried everywhere they could enter the pipeline. Spend the collection budget on
a \emph{new environment} instead.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}
