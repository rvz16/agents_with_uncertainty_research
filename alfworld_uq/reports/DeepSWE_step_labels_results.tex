\title{PRR: DeepSWE with per-step labels (verifier-graded trajectory prefixes)}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-8mm}
{\small Per-step labels | DeepSWE mini-swe-agent, working-tree grading | 2 label settings \ensuremath{\times} 2 prefix slicings | in-domain | 2026-11-07\par}
\begin{itemize}\small
\item \textbf{Per-step labels.} For a sampled set of steps $k$ of each episode the repository was checked out \emph{as it actually stood} after command $k$ and the task's hidden tests were re-run, giving the verifier's native partial-credit score (F2P + P2P passed / all) at that step. The prefix of commands $0..k$ becomes a scored unit. 282 graded points over 79 episodes on DG, 332 over 91 on DQ.
\item \textbf{The two labels.} \emph{Step label} is that re-run score at step $k$. \emph{Final label} is the episode's own terminal partial score stamped on the same prefix --- the labelling every table in the main report uses. Both arms share prefixes, folds, signals, critics and code; only the label's value changes.
\item \textbf{The two slicings.} \emph{Graded} keeps only the steps a verifier actually re-ran. \emph{Carried} propagates a score forward over the commands that did not change the repository, which is exact rather than an approximation, and multiplies the unit count by 5.9\ensuremath{\times} on DG and 14.8\ensuremath{\times} on DQ. Carried numbers are therefore unequally replicated across episodes and are reported for continuity with the pre-2026-11-04 tables, not as the headline.
\item \textbf{Folds.} 5-fold out-of-fold evaluation cut by episode (\texttt{numpy.random.RandomState}, seeds as in the main report); all prefixes of an episode sit in the same fold, so no episode crosses train and test. PRR on the pooled held-out predictions, averaged over seeds.
\item \textbf{Scores are continuous.} Neither model resolves a DeepSWE task outright (binary reward 0/113 for both), so PRR uses the partial score without binarisation: the oracle ranks by the true score, the random reference is the mean score. Bayes and regression need a binary training label; every training fold is split at its own median.
\item \textbf{Critics are cut to the prefix.} Tool critics and the tool success rate are recomputed from the commands inside each prefix. The earlier tables stamped whole-episode critics on every step, which let every critic-based method see the future; those absolute numbers are withdrawn and are not reproduced here.
\item \textbf{Base rates differ between the arms.} Mean step label is 0.27 (DG) and 0.40 (DQ) against terminal 0.39 and 0.77 on the graded slicing. PRR's random reference is the mean score, so the two columns are not measuring one fixed task --- a point in favour of reading the Spearman agreement rather than the level.
\item \textbf{Two methods cannot be computed on this benchmark at all, for data reasons.} HTC needs per-token confidence sequences; the DeepSWE records carry only per-command aggregates (\texttt{num\_tokens}, \texttt{mean\_logprob}, \texttt{min\_logprob}, \texttt{mean\_entropy}, \texttt{max\_entropy}, \texttt{self\_certainty}) and no token lists, so \texttt{htc\_tokens} is absent and every HTC variant returns nothing. All5 Bayes Fused needs a Verb-actions signal, and verbalised confidence was requested in the system prompt and ignored by both models (Qwen 0/1806 commands, gpt-oss 49/6365), so there is no fifth signal to fuse; the four single-signal Bayes Fused History + Last rows are reported instead. Neither absence is a build problem and neither can be closed without re-collecting the runs.
\item \textbf{Logistic regression carries a caveat.} The ridge fitter emits overflow and invalid-value warnings on some folds of this data. The resulting numbers are in line with the whole-episode report on the same cohorts (there: selected $+0.32$ / $-0.02$, pinned $+0.36$ / $-0.02$), so the fit is not degenerate, but the rows should not be quoted to the second decimal.
\item \textbf{Ranks} are descriptive, by unrounded Avg of \textbf{Step label, graded} within each table, which is the one arm that is neither replicated nor hindsight.
\end{itemize}
{\small DG: DeepSWE gpt-oss-20b. DQ: DeepSWE Qwen3.6-35B. Actor harness: mini-swe-agent, 200-command budget.\par}

\section*{Training on steps, testing on whole trajectories}
{\small A \emph{final} unit is the whole pre-terminal trajectory carrying the episode's own
verifier score --- the unit the main report scores. A \emph{step} unit is a trajectory cut at a
step the verifier re-ran, carrying that step's score. Folds are cut by episode, so a trajectory
and all of its own cuts always sit on the same side of the split.\par}

\section*{Reading the step-trained columns}
\begin{itemize}\small
\item \textbf{Training on steps and testing on whole trajectories is about as good as the
ordinary setup, and no better.} $+0.133$ against $+0.123$, a gain of $+0.010$ on 72 methods. The
per-cohort signs disagree as everywhere else in this report: $+0.163$ against $+0.106$ on DG,
$+0.103$ against $+0.141$ on DQ. This agrees with the BrowseComp-Plus step-label report, where
\emph{Train all \ensuremath{\to} test final} and \emph{Train final \ensuremath{\to} test final}
also coincide.
\item \textbf{A first measurement of ours was an artefact of the fold split, and is withdrawn.}
It read $+0.147$ against $+0.101$. \texttt{folds\_by\_episode} strata on the mean score of an
episode's units, so the two label arms were scored on different partitions and a quantity that
cannot depend on the prefixes' labels at all --- final \ensuremath{\to} final --- moved by
$0.022$. Every number here uses folds stratified on the episode's own terminal score instead,
which makes that baseline identical across arms bit-for-bit.
\item \textbf{What does survive is about the label, not the volume.} With prefixes in the
training set, scoring them by the verifier's own reading beats stamping the final outcome on them,
by $+0.036$ (train on steps) and $+0.034$ (train on all). Relabelling the prefixes by the outcome
is worse than not using them at all ($+0.097$ against the $+0.123$ baseline). So if one augments
with prefixes, the step label is the right label --- which is a narrower claim than "collect step
labels": it does not improve prefix-level prediction, where this report began.
\item \textbf{Why the prefixes can help here at all.} Finals are scarce --- 99 and 65 episodes
against 271 and 202 prefixes --- so adding prefixes multiplies the training set by about three.
On BrowseComp-Plus the finals number 702 and 743, and prefixes add proportionally much less, which
is the likeliest reason adding them changes nothing there.
\item \textbf{One caution on train \ensuremath{\to} test shift.} In train steps
\ensuremath{\to} test finals the training units sit at 85\,\% (DG) and 81\,\% (DQ) of episode
length on average and carry a mean label of 0.27 and 0.40, while the test units are whole
trajectories with a mean of 0.39 and 0.77. The model is fitted on systematically more pessimistic
labels than it is scored against, so a shortfall in this column is first of all a base-rate shift.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Reading}
\begin{itemize}\small
\item \textbf{The labels genuinely disagree, and far more than on DG alone.} 36\,\% of graded points on DG and \textbf{78\,\%} on DQ carry a step label different from the episode's outcome; 14 points on DG and 19 on DQ are \emph{above} it, i.e.\ the agent had more hidden tests passing mid-run than it finished with. The premise behind collecting per-step labels is correct.
\item \textbf{It buys nothing on average.} Swapping the label moves the mean PRR by $+0.034$ (graded) and $+0.012$ (carried). Both are inside the spread between cohorts and the sign is not stable: on DG the step label helps carried and hurts graded, on DQ exactly the reverse.
\item \textbf{Where it does matter, it matters by disagreeing with itself.} On DG the two labels rank the 72 methods almost identically ($\rho=0.70$--$0.72$); on DQ the ranking \emph{inverts} ($\rho=-0.36$ / $-0.43$). A target that reorders the method table in opposite directions on two cohorts of the same benchmark is not a better target; it is a second noisy view.
\item \textbf{Carrying is replication, not information.} It multiplies units $5.9\times$ on DG and $14.8\times$ on DQ, unequally across episodes. Any gain measured there is partly the duplication, which is how a $+0.23$ in an earlier table shrank to $+0.04$ on graded points only.
\item \textbf{Coverage is biased on DG only.} There the ungraded episodes are the short successful ones (mean 0.722 and 29 commands against 0.414 and 64); on DQ the outcome is the same either way (0.713 against 0.748) and the ungraded episodes are longer (152 commands against 128). Within-slicing comparison is sound on both; no absolute DG number may sit beside the main table. The ranking-inversion result rests on DQ, which is the cohort whose coverage is not biased.
\item \textbf{Agreement with the BrowseComp-Plus step-label report.} There, \emph{Train all \ensuremath{\to} test final} and \emph{Train final \ensuremath{\to} test final} coincide to within $0.01$--$0.02$ for every reference method (Logistic regression $0.94$ vs $0.95$, HTC $0.90$ vs $0.92$, B4 $0.87$ vs $0.90$, Verb max $0.94$ vs $0.94$) --- adding prefixes with their own step labels to training does not improve the forecast, which is the same conclusion. That report also warns that its \emph{test all} column is inflated by answer length (Logprob last $0.90$ there against $0.50$ on finals); our analogue is the carried slicing, inflated by replication. The levels differ ($0.85$--$0.96$ there against a best Avg of $+0.29$ here) because BrowseComp grades binary success while DeepSWE gives partial credit on tasks neither model resolves outright, and because this environment offers no progress signal: process critics saturate and failing test runs are how the work proceeds, so the tool success rate is negative on DG.
\end{itemize}
